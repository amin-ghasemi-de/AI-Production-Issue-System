DOCUMENT_TYPE: severity
TITLE: Production Issue Severity Rules for Injection Molding RAG
PURPOSE: Ground LOW, MEDIUM, HIGH, or null severity in documented evidence.
IMPORTANT: This is a project-specific PoC classification policy derived from machinery-safety and injection-molding troubleshooting guidance. It is NOT a universal industry severity standard. Factory-approved risk criteria, machine manuals, material data sheets, and process windows must override this file when available.

RULE S-01 — Safety override to HIGH
If the report describes a credible risk of crushing, amputation, electric shock, burns from hot plastic or hot machine surfaces, high-pressure fluid spray, fire, or exposure to hazardous machine energy, classify severity as HIGH.
Evidence basis: OSHA identifies crushing, amputation, electric shock, burns, high-temperature exposure, and high-pressure hose or fitting failures as serious hazards around injection molding machinery.

RULE S-02 — Guard or interlock bypass is HIGH
If the report states that a guard, operator gate, interlock, or safety device is missing, bypassed, defeated, or intentionally overridden while the machine can operate, classify severity as HIGH.
Evidence basis: OSHA injection-molding guidance warns not to operate with missing or inoperable guards or interlocks and not to bypass safety interlocks.

RULE S-03 — Servicing with hazardous energy exposure is HIGH
If maintenance, cleaning, unjamming, mold access, inspection, adjustment, or servicing requires removal or bypass of a guard or entry into a danger zone while hazardous energy could be released, classify severity as HIGH.
Evidence basis: OSHA 29 CFR 1910.147 guidance requires lockout/tagout when servicing exposes workers to unexpected energization, startup, or hazardous-energy release.

RULE S-04 — High-pressure hose or fitting failure is HIGH
If the report mentions a damaged, punctured, frayed, leaking, or failed hydraulic hose or fitting that can spray hot oil or another substance at high pressure, classify severity as HIGH.
Evidence basis: OSHA states that damaged hoses and fittings can spray hot oil or other substances at high pressure and can create injury and fire hazards.

RULE S-05 — Thermal contact or molten-plastic exposure is HIGH when personnel exposure is credible
If the report describes splatter of molten plastic, contact with a heated barrel, hot nozzle, or another thermal hazard with credible worker exposure, classify severity as HIGH.
Evidence basis: OSHA identifies burns from hot barrels and splatter of hot plastic, gases, or vapors as injection-molding hazards.

RULE S-06 — Confirmed production defect without documented safety hazard is MEDIUM under this project policy
If a report clearly describes a repeatable production-quality defect such as short shots, flash, burn marks, voids, sink marks, warpage, splay, off-color parts, dimensional out-of-specification parts, or repeated sticking/ejection problems, and no safety-critical condition is documented, classify severity as MEDIUM.
Policy rationale: Manufacturer troubleshooting guides treat these as process or quality-control problems requiring corrective intervention. This MEDIUM mapping is a project policy for the PoC, not a manufacturer or ISO severity rating.

RULE S-07 — Persistent or worsening process instability is MEDIUM under this project policy
If the report states that an issue is recurring, progressively worsening, or repeatedly producing nonconforming parts, and no HIGH safety rule applies, classify severity as MEDIUM.
Evidence basis: Avient distinguishes quality-control issues as cases where previously successful production becomes out of specification because something in the process changed. Solvay documents progressively shorter shots as a troubleshooting condition requiring correction.

RULE S-08 — Minor isolated cosmetic deviation may be LOW only when functional and safety impact are explicitly absent
If the report explicitly describes a minor, isolated cosmetic variation and also states that the part remains functionally acceptable and no safety hazard is present, classify severity as LOW.
Examples may include limited appearance variation such as gloss variation or minor surface appearance issues when the report explicitly confirms no functional impact.
Policy note: Do not infer LOW merely because a defect sounds cosmetic; the report must explicitly support the lack of functional and safety impact.

RULE S-09 — Numeric process values alone do not determine severity
Do not classify severity from temperature, pressure, cycle time, or downtime numbers alone unless a retrieved machine manual, material processing guide, approved process window, or factory rule explicitly defines a limit or acceptable range for the same parameter.
Examples: Temperature 95, pressure 120, or cycle time 48 must not be labeled LOW, MEDIUM, or HIGH without the correct unit, parameter meaning, material/machine context, and documented limit.

RULE S-10 — Material process windows are material-specific
Do not assume that a temperature, pressure, injection time, cooling time, or cycle time that is acceptable for one resin is valid for another resin.
Evidence basis: Avient recommends determining the material process window before troubleshooting. BASF cycle-time guidance depends on material type, melt temperature, mold temperature, wall thickness, cooling time, and other process factors.

RULE S-11 — Downtime does not determine severity without a factory rule
Do not map downtime minutes directly to LOW, MEDIUM, or HIGH unless the factory provides explicit downtime thresholds or escalation criteria.
Reason: The researched sources support risk assessment and process troubleshooting but do not establish a universal downtime-to-severity mapping for injection molding.

RULE S-12 — Insufficient evidence returns null
Return severity as null when the report contains only vague statements, unsupported numeric values, or insufficient context to apply one of the documented rules.
Do not guess severity.

RULE S-13 — Safety evidence takes precedence over quality evidence
If both a quality defect and a credible safety hazard are present, apply the safety rule first. A documented safety-critical condition must not be downgraded because the report also contains a routine quality defect.
Basis: ISO 12100 describes machinery risk assessment as hazard identification, risk estimation/evaluation, and risk reduction. OSHA documents serious injection-molding hazards including amputation, crushing, electric shock, burns, and hazardous-energy exposure.
